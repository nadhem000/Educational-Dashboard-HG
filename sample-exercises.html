1. Define the following terms: Cold War, Iron Curtain.
Cold War:
state of political hostility between the USA and USSR without direct military conflict.
Iron Curtain:
the ideological and physical division of Europe after WWII.

4. Analyse the causes of the Berlin Blockade (1948-1949) in one paragraph.
Analyse:
Causes of the Berlin Blockade: - Introduction of a new currency in West Germany. - Western Allies' refusal to allow the Soviets to control all of Berlin. - The USSR aimed to force the Western powers out of Berlin. - The Western Allies responded with the Berlin Airlift, demonstrating resolve.
Paragraph:
The Berlin Blockade was triggered by the introduction of a new currency in West Germany and the Western Allies’ refusal to allow the Soviets to control all of Berlin. The USSR hoped to force the West out, but the Western Allies responded with the Berlin Airlift, a successful display of resolve.
5. Write a short article (150 words) on the impact of the Cuban Missile Crisis on US-Soviet relations.
Analyse:
The Cuban Missile Crisis brought the US and USSR to the brink of nuclear war. To understand its impact, analyse the immediate crisis management measures (hotline, test ban) and the long-term shift toward détente and arms control.
Plan:
  • Introduction: Present the crisis as the Cold War’s most dangerous moment.
  • Development: I. Crisis Management (Hotline, Test Ban Treaty); II. Towards Détente (Nuclear taboo, arms control legacy).
  • Conclusion: From brinkmanship to guarded cooperation.
Production:

Introduction

The Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962 was the Cold War’s most perilous moment, bringing the United States and the Soviet Union to the edge of nuclear war. Its shock fundamentally transformed their adversarial relationship.

Development

I. Crisis Management

I.1. Hotline

In 1963, the Washington-Moscow hotline was established, providing a direct teletype link to prevent misunderstandings during emergencies.

I.2. Test Ban Treaty

The same year, the Limited Test Ban Treaty prohibited nuclear tests in the atmosphere, outer space, and underwater, signalling a new willingness to limit the arms race.

II. Towards Détente

II.1. Nuclear Taboo

The brush with annihilation cemented mutual acceptance of mutually assured destruction, making deliberate nuclear confrontation taboo.

II.2. Arms Control Legacy

This recognition later fostered the 1972 SALT I agreements and a permanent culture of superpower summitry.

Conclusion

Ultimately, the crisis replaced reckless brinkmanship with a framework of guarded cooperation, embedding arms control and crisis communication into the superpower rivalry for decades to come.
